Krillion Strategy: How to Find Rarer Answers

Krillion rewards a specific skill: producing a valid answer that is less obvious than everyone else’s first thought. These are the habits that reliably add depth, plus the traps that quietly cost points. Every example below comes from our own practice bank, not from a real Krillion dive.

The maths that should guide you

Seven average answers beat one brilliant answer and six blanks. Rare answers score 60 points; Plankton scores 10. A player who lands seven Rare answers finishes at 420 points (4,200 m). A player who gambles for the 100-point gem and misses three prompts finishes at 400 points at best. Your floor matters as much as your ceiling.

1. Read the prompt as a constraint, not a topic

“Name a country that starts with S” is not “name a country”. The letter is the whole puzzle. Underline the qualifier in your head: a starting letter, a region, a time period, a medium. Then generate answers that satisfy every part.

Drill: take a prompt and say the constraints out loud before you think of an answer.

2. Generate three candidates before you type

The first answer you think of is usually the most common one — that is what makes it first. Spend three seconds listing two more valid candidates, then choose the least obvious one you can spell. This single habit moves most players from Plankton toward Schooler and Rare.

Example (our bank): “Name a pizza topping.” First thought: pepperoni (common). Second: mushroom (common). Third: artichoke (much rarer). Choose the third if you are confident it is valid.

3. Step sideways within the category

Obvious answers cluster around the same subcategories. If a prompt asks for a movie with a heist, most players name Hollywood blockbusters. Step sideways: older films, non-English films, documentaries, animated films. The category stays valid; the answer becomes less crowded.

4. Watch for the Too Clever trap

Too Clever is a real tier, and it is worth only 15 points — five more than Plankton. It catches the “famous obscure” answer: the pick that feels like a deep cut because it is quirky, but that thousands of other players also reach for. If your answer is a well-known piece of trivia, it is probably Too Clever, not Deep Cut.

Ask yourself: “Would this answer appear in a listicle called ‘obscure facts about X’?” If yes, keep looking.

5. Specificity is not the same as obscurity

A specific answer is only deeper if it is still valid and less commonly named. Naming a tiny village when the prompt asks for a city can fail the validator; naming a mid-sized city that fits the prompt exactly can score well. Precision beats weirdness.

6. Protect your 25 seconds

A practical split: 4 seconds reading and checking the qualifier, 9 seconds generating two or three candidates, 8 seconds typing and checking the spelling, 4 seconds buffer to submit. The buffer matters: a great answer submitted after the timer is worth zero.

7. Spelling discipline

The validator is generous with aliases, but a name you cannot spell is a risk you do not need. Prefer an answer you can type cleanly over a marginally rarer one you will mangle. On a phone, autocorrect can help or hurt; check the word before you submit.

8. Build a personal depth list

After each dive, note the prompts where you scored Plankton or missed. For each, write two less obvious valid answers you could have used. Ten minutes of review beats another ten random dives, because you are training recall for categories that actually appear.

9. Practise with a timer

Untimed practice builds knowledge; timed practice builds the skill the game actually scores. Our Practice Dive gives you seven prompts and 25 seconds each, with an original answer bank. Use it for one focused round a day: pick a single rule (three candidates, or sideways subcategories) and apply it to every prompt.

10. Review the distribution, not just the total

A 350-point run built from seven Schooler answers is a more stable strategy than a 350-point run with one 100-point gem and three zeros. Over a month, the stable distribution wins. Track how many prompts you leave blank — that number is the easiest one to fix.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best strategy for Krillion?

Generate two or three valid answers before typing, choose the least obvious one you can spell, and never leave a prompt blank. Consistency across seven prompts beats chasing one rare answer.

What is the Too Clever tier?

A 15-point tier for the famous ‘obscure’ answer that many players still name. It is only five points above Plankton, so it is a trap rather than a win.

Should I guess in Krillion?

No. An invalid guess scores zero, and the timer keeps running. If you are unsure, name a valid answer you can spell rather than a risky one.
